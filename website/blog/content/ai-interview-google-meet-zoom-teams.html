To run an AI interview in Google Meet, Zoom or Teams, you send a meeting bot into the candidate's call and connect a voice agent to it. The bot joins like any participant, someone admits it from the lobby, and the agent talks to the candidate through it. In our Google Meet tests the bot was in the meeting about 17 to 23 seconds after launch.

The hard parts aren't in the call itself. They're around it: making your app reachable from the internet, choosing voice providers that won't run out of quota mid-interview, and handling each platform's rules for who can join.

A meeting bot is a server-side participant that a provider runs for you. It joins by link, streams audio both ways and reports events, such as being admitted or the call ending, back to your app through webhooks.

In this article, we'll explore how the bot and the voice agent fit together, what has to be reachable from the internet, platform-by-platform rules for Meet, Zoom and Teams, a step-by-step setup, and the failures you'll most likely meet. Let's get started!

How does an AI interviewer get into a meeting?

Three pieces work together. Your app holds the interview: the approved questions, the consent rule and the record. A meeting bot provider runs the participant that joins the call. A voice agent, hosted by that provider, listens to the candidate and speaks.

Recruence uses MeetStream for the last two pieces. When an interview starts, Recruence creates a voice agent configuration (the providers, the instructions and two tools, next_question and end_interview), then asks MeetStream to send a bot to the meeting link with that agent. The structured AI interviewer article explains why the questions come through a tool instead of the instructions.

What has to be reachable from the internet?

This is the step that trips up most first attempts. During the call, MeetStream calls your app: the agent fetches each question from /webhooks/mia/…/next_question, and the bot reports events and the live transcript to /webhooks/meetstream/…. Candidates also open their interview link on your app. All of that needs a public HTTPS address that leads to your app.

  • For a quick test, a Cloudflare quick tunnel gives your local app a temporary https://….trycloudflare.com address.
  • For real use, a domain with a reverse proxy, or a named tunnel, so the address doesn't change on every restart.
# Recruence desktop app listens on 127.0.0.1:8765
cloudflared tunnel --url http://127.0.0.1:8765
A common mistake

A tunnel that leads to another app on the same machine, say your careers website, resolves and answers, but MeetStream's calls never reach the interview. Recruence's Public URL check says exactly that: it reports the HTTP status the address returned and whether it's this app, another copy of Recruence, or something else.

Only the candidate-facing and webhook paths answer through the public address; the recruiter dashboard stays on your own machine or network.

What are the rules for Google Meet, Zoom and Teams?

Each platform decides who may join and how. These are the rules that mattered in our live tests:

PlatformWhat the bot needsWho admits itNotes from testing
Google MeetA Meet link; nothing extraAnyone in the call, from the lobbyFull interviews ran end to end. Start the meeting before the bot arrives.
Microsoft TeamsA guest-joinable meeting linkThe organiser or a participantWorked end to end with consumer teams.live.com meetings.
ZoomA Zoom Meeting SDK app set up in MeetStreamThe host, if a waiting room is onThe meeting must belong to that app's Zoom account (Zoom Meeting SDK).

For a self-serve interview, a reusable room per role works well: create one Google Meet with a long time window (for example a calendar event spanning the interview week) and use its link as the role's interview room. Recruence books the room one interview at a time, so two candidates never land in it together. Google explains how to create a meeting for later in its Meet help.

Which voice providers should you choose?

A pipeline voice agent has three parts: speech-to-text, a conversation model and a voice. MeetStream supports several providers for each, and you can mix them. The constraint is that each provider's key lives in MeetStream's Integrations, and each one has its own limits.

SetupSpeech-to-text → conversation → voiceResult in our tests
All xAIxAI → Grok → xAI voiceWorked end to end on Meet and Teams
Gemini pipelineDeepgram → Gemini → ElevenLabsWorked end to end on Meet with gemini-3.6-flash
Gemini free tierSame, free quotaStopped after two questions: 20 requests a day per model
Gemini Live (realtime)One model hears, thinks and speaksUnreliable tool calls; not ready for interviews yet

Each turn of the conversation is a model request, so a 20-minute interview easily needs forty or more. Size your quota for that before a candidate is on the call.

How Recruence handles the call for you

Recruence wires the pieces together so a recruiter only deals with links. A role has one interview room; each eligible candidate gets a personal interview link; the candidate opens it, confirms a one-time code sent to their email, and presses Start. The bot joins the role's room, and the interview follows the role's approved questions.

Around the call, it does the checks that save a failed interview:

  • Before launch: the Public URL must resolve and lead to this app, and the voice setup must cover speech-to-text, conversation and voice.
  • During the call: the live transcript and interviewer notes appear in the dashboard; if the agent reports an error and doesn't recover within two minutes, the interview is ended cleanly.
  • After the call: the bot leaves, its agent configuration is deleted, the transcript is rebuilt from the recording, and the evidence-based scorecard is written.

Prefer to drive it from your own code? The same flow is an HTTP API; starting an interview that's ready is one call:

curl -X POST https://your-recruence.example/api/interviews/$INTERVIEW_ID/launch \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $APP_ACCESS_TOKEN" -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"meeting_link": "https://meet.google.com/abc-defg-hij", "consent_attested": true}'

How do you set up your first AI interview?

  1. Create a MeetStream API key and add your voice providers' keys in MeetStream's Integrations.
  2. Install Recruence (desktop app or Docker) and finish the setup: database, account, AI provider, MeetStream key.
  3. Give it a public address: start a tunnel or point your domain at it, save it as the Public URL, and run the check until it says it reaches this app.
  4. Set up email so candidates receive their interview link and the one-time code.
  5. Create a role, approve its questions, and set the interview room to your Meet, Zoom or Teams link.
  6. Interview yourself first. Apply, open the link, admit the bot, and answer with headphones on.
  7. Read the server log after the test, even if it went well. Agent errors, lobby timeouts and quota warnings all show up there.

The interview setup guide covers each step with screenshots of the settings.

What to take away about running AI interviews in meetings

Getting an AI interviewer into Google Meet, Zoom or Teams is mostly about what surrounds the call: a public address that leads to the right app, voice providers with enough quota for a whole interview, and each platform's joining rules. Get those right, and the interview itself is the easy part.

If you want to skip the wiring, Recruence's desktop app runs on Windows, macOS and Linux and handles the bot, the links and the checks for you. Start with the latest release.
